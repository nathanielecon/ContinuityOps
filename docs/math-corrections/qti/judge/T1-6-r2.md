VERIFICATION OF THE THREE THINGS THE COORDINATOR ASKED ABOUT

1. Does the parenthesised form resolve the double-signing, or merely paper over it?
   It resolves it. The two forms differ in what part of speech the direction words
   occupy, and that is a substantive difference, not a cosmetic one:

     Old form -- "-180 feet below sea level" -- puts "below sea level" as a bare
     postmodifier inside the same noun phrase as the signed quantity. It therefore
     predicates position of the value -180 feet, and the two sign-bearing operators
     compose: negative value x below-datum = 180 feet ABOVE sea level. Two direction
     assertions, multiplied. The keyed choice was literally false.

     New form -- "-180 feet (below sea level)" -- sets the direction words off as a
     parenthetical gloss. A parenthetical here is metalinguistic: it names what the
     minus sign already encodes rather than applying a second displacement to it.
     The string carries exactly ONE direction assertion, the sign, and the
     parenthesis annotates it. Compose nothing, contradict nothing.

   Two independent confirmations that this is the right target and not an
   author's convenience:
     - It is the answer key's own text. Topic1Part1Solutions.pdf p.4, read
       rendered, prints "Answer: Part A: -15; Part B: -180 feet (below sea level)".
       The QTI choice is now character-identical to it, modulo ASCII hyphen-minus
       vs the PDF's typographic minus -- and ASCII hyphen is this package's uniform
       convention (Part A: -15, -1 kilometer, -25, -36 ...). Criterion 4 key
       agreement is now exact rather than approximate.
     - The arithmetic is unchanged and still checks: (-15) ft/min x 12 min = -180 ft,
       magnitude 180, direction below. Recomputed.

   The alternative repair -- copying Part 2's unsigned phrasing to give
   "Part B: 180 feet below sea level" -- would have been worse: it drops the sign
   that Part A of the same item establishes and that the key prints. The fixer
   took the correct branch.
